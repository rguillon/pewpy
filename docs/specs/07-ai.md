# 07 — AI player

An AI that learns to play the game by itself, one neural network ("brain") flying every player ship (it sees which
one it flies), and uses what it learned to rate how hard each level is for each ship. Placeholders chosen by Claude unless said otherwise; not tuned on
long runs yet *(placeholder)*.

## Modes

- **AI learning** (the dev tools' main menu, `make dev`, or `make learn` without a window): the brain learns on every level with every
  ship, until stopped (Escape in the game; `--generations` on the command line). On screen, the brain (as it is so
  far) flies a ship drawn at random on a level drawn at random, another one when it ends, over a panel with its
  generation, its best try's fitness, the worlds it trains on, and for each ship the share of the levels it clears
  (with one life) and how far into them it gets on average. What it learned is kept: learning goes on from there next time.
- **Learning on chosen levels** (`make learn-level1`: level 1-1 only; `make learn-random`: every level, drawn at
  random; `--levels 1-1 2-3 …` on the command line): no curriculum, the brain trains on those levels only, and
  each check plays them 48 times in all with each ship (each level as often). `--new` starts a new brain instead of
  going on with the saved one.
- **AI rating** (the dev tools' main menu, or `make rate`): the brain, if there is one, flies each ship on every level by the game's rules
  (all its lives, a death restarts the level, the weapons at level 1, as when a level is picked from the menu),
  10 times with different randomness. The rating is the **clear rate**: the share of those runs that cleared
  the level, in % *(the user's choice)*: the lower, the harder. The game shows the table filling up, levels down
  and ships across (worlds 1 to 4, then 5 to 8 side by side); the ratings are saved with each run's average
  progress and lives lost. Without any brain, the screen says to start AI learning first.
- **AI playing** (the dev tools' main menu): the saved brain (a new one if there is none) plays the game on screen,
  to watch it: by the game's rules (all its lives, a death restarts the level), from level 1-1, going on to the next
  level with its score, lives and weapons once one is cleared; at game over, a new game with the next ship (the
  brain loaded again: it may have learned since). Over it, the brain's generation, what is on screen, and for each
  ship the most levels it cleared in a game. Nothing is learned or saved.
- Learning and rating work in the background, in worker processes (the cores but two in the game, the cores but one on the
  command line); the game keeps running.

## Files

- In the user's data folder: `~/.local/share/pewpy/ai/` (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\pewpy\ai\` on Windows): `brain.npz`,
  the brain with its generation and its training's history (every 10 generations: the best and mean fitness, the
  worlds open, and for each ship the share of levels cleared and how far into them it got), and `ratings.json`, the
  last complete rating (the date, the runs per level, the brain's generation, for each ship each level's place,
  name, clear rate, progress and lives lost).
- A brain made for other sensors (they changed) is ignored: learning starts again.

## What the AI sees (`pewpy.tools.ai.sensors`)

- A danger radar that plans ahead: for each of 9 moves (8 directions and staying put), the ship flies it for 0.25 s,
  then the best of the 9 moves until 0.8 s (with the ship's inertia; the enemy shots and enemies within 0.9 world
  units going on at their speed, a snaking shot as wide as its snaking): how long before a hit (closer than 0.02
  world units; 1 for none) and how much room is left on the way (from 0 to 1, 0.2 world units or more). From these,
  the safest move (its direction) and the **move to aim**: among the safe moves (scoring nearly as well as the
  safest), the one that brings the ship to its target: onto the nearest pickup (where it will be in 0.25 s; when the
  pickup is above the middle of the screen, under it, waiting for it to drift down), else under the boss (under its
  part nearest across while its core is armored), else under the nearest enemy above it on screen, near the middle
  of the screen's height *(placeholder: 0.0, minded 0.3 times less than being under the target)*.
- The 6 nearest enemy shots: where they are from the ship, how fast they go.
- 8 lanes across the screen: how many enemy shots and enemies in each, above the ship.
- The ship: where it is, its speed, health, whether it is invulnerable, its kind (its top speed and size, and
  which ship it is: one input per ship, 1 for the one it flies); the weapon
  selected and the three weapons' levels.
- The 3 nearest enemies and the nearest pickup (where they are), the boss (where, its health).

## What it does (`pewpy.tools.ai.brain`, `pewpy.tools.ai.pilot`)

- A small neural network: one hidden layer of 24 neurons (tanh), and a direct path from the inputs to the
  outputs; 6 outputs: the stick (x and y, from -1 to 1), the fire button (pressed above 0), and how much it wants
  each weapon (the highest is selected).
- It decides 30 times a second and holds the stick in between, like a player's reaction time.
- A new brain starts with random weights (ten times smaller to the stick), the fire button held, the direct path
  closed but from the radar's move to aim to the stick (3 times over, the stick then clamped) and from the weapons'
  levels to the wanting of each (10 times over), the bullets wanted a little more: it dodges, aims, picks up the
  pickups and fires its best weapon (the bullets when tied) before it has learned anything.

## How it learns (`pewpy.tools.ai.evolution`, `pewpy.tools.ai.learning`, `pewpy.tools.ai.episode`)

- Evolution strategies (OpenAI's ES), no other library than numpy: each generation tries the brain's weights
  with 32 random nudges and their opposites (64 tries), each try playing the same runs, one per ship, each on a
  level drawn among the open worlds' levels (with the same randomness), with one life; the brain moves towards the nudges that did better (ranked, so
  only the order counts), with small Adam steps (nudges of 0.02, steps of 0.01 *(placeholder)*: bigger ones lose
  the brain it starts from) and a little weight decay.
- A try's fitness is its runs' average, each run counting: how far it got in seconds on the waves' clock (which
  waits while a boss is fought: dodging a boss without destroying it earns nothing), its score (1 per 250 points),
  the pickups it took (5 each *(placeholder)*: the weapons' upgrades carry on to the next levels),
  how much of each boss it destroyed, the mini boss as the final one (up to 60 each), and clearing the level (150,
  plus up to 30 for the health left). A run gives up 60 s per boss after the final boss is due *(placeholder)*.
- Every 10 generations the brain plays every level once with each ship, with one life: the share it clears and how far it gets
  on average (to the final boss: half way; the final boss destroyed: all the way) are its progress; it is saved then.
- A curriculum: a new brain trains on world 1 only; at each of those checks, once it gets half way into the open
  worlds' levels on average over every ship (to their final boss), the next world opens *(placeholder)*. The worlds open are saved
  with the brain.
