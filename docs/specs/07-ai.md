# 07 — AI player

An AI that learns to play the game by itself, one neural network ("brain") per player ship, and uses what it learned
to rate how hard each level is for each ship. Placeholders chosen by Claude unless said otherwise; not tuned on
long runs yet *(placeholder)*.

## Modes

- **AI learning** (main menu, or `make learn` without a window): every ship's brain learns on every level, the
  ships taking turns (10 generations each), until stopped (Escape in the game; `--generations` on the command
  line). On screen, the brain of the ship learning now (as it is so far) plays a level drawn at random, another
  one when it ends, over a panel with each ship's generation, its best try's fitness, the share of the levels
  its brain clears (with one life) and how far into them it gets on average. What it learned is kept: learning goes on from there next time.
- **AI rating** (main menu, or `make rate`): each ship that has a brain plays every level by the game's rules
  (all its lives, a death restarts the level, the weapons at level 1, as when a level is picked from the menu),
  10 times with different randomness. The rating is the **clear rate**: the share of those runs that cleared
  the level, in % *(the user's choice)*: the lower, the harder. The game shows the table filling up, levels down
  and ships across (worlds 1 to 4, then 5 to 8 side by side); the ratings are saved with each run's average
  progress and lives lost. Without any brain, the screen says to start AI learning first.
- Both work in the background, in worker processes (the cores but two in the game, the cores but one on the
  command line); the game keeps running.

## Files

- In the user's data folder: `~/.local/share/pewpy/ai/` (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\pewpy\ai\` on Windows): `<ship>.npz`,
  each ship's brain with its generation and its training's history (every 10 generations: the best and mean
  fitness, the share of levels cleared, how far into them it got), and `ratings.json`, the last complete rating (the date, the runs per
  level, for each ship its brain's generation and each level's place, name, clear rate, progress and lives lost).
- A brain made for other sensors (they changed) is ignored: that ship starts learning again.

## What the AI sees (`pewpy.ai.sensors`)

- A danger radar: for each of 9 moves (8 directions and staying put), how much room is left between the ship and
  the nearest enemy shot or enemy if it flew that way for 0.3 s and for 0.7 s, everything going on at its speed
  (from 0, a hit, to 1, 0.2 world units or more), and the safest of the moves (its direction).
- The 6 nearest enemy shots: where they are from the ship, how fast they go.
- 8 lanes across the screen: how many enemy shots and enemies in each, above the ship.
- The ship: where it is, its speed, health, whether it is invulnerable, its kind (speed, size); the weapon
  selected and the three weapons' levels.
- The 3 nearest enemies and the nearest pickup (where they are), the boss (where, its health).

## What it does (`pewpy.ai.brain`, `pewpy.ai.pilot`)

- A small neural network: one hidden layer of 24 neurons (tanh), and a direct path from the inputs to the
  outputs; 6 outputs: the stick (x and y, from -1 to 1), the fire button (pressed above 0), and how much it wants
  each weapon (the highest is selected).
- It decides 30 times a second and holds the stick in between, like a player's reaction time.
- A new brain starts with random weights, the fire button held and the direct path closed.

## How it learns (`pewpy.ai.evolution`, `pewpy.ai.learning`, `pewpy.ai.episode`)

- Evolution strategies (OpenAI's ES), no other library than numpy: each generation tries the brain's weights
  with 32 random nudges and their opposites (64 tries), each try playing the same 3 levels (drawn among all of
  them, with the same randomness) with one life; the brain moves towards the nudges that did better (ranked, so
  only the order counts), with Adam steps and a little weight decay.
- A try's fitness is its runs' average, each run counting: the seconds it lasted, its score (1 per 250 points),
  how much of the boss it destroyed (up to 60) and clearing the level (150, plus up to 30 for the health left).
  A run gives up 90 s after the boss comes.
- Every 10 generations the brain plays every level once with one life: the share it clears and how far it gets
  on average (to the boss: half way; the boss destroyed: all the way) are its progress; it is saved then.
